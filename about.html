🕰️ A Brief History of Indian Railways

Indian Railways traces its origins to the mid-19th century, when the British colonial administration built the first lines primarily to move troops, mail, and raw materials. What began as a handful of experimental tracks grew, over more than 170 years, into one of the largest railway systems ever built by a single organisation.

1853
India's first passenger train ran between Bombay (Bori Bunder) and Thane, a distance of about 34 km, on 16 April 1853 — marking the birth of railways in India.
1854
The Eastern Indian Railway Company opened the Howrah–Hooghly line in Bengal, extending the network into eastern India.
1951
Indian Railways was nationalised, bringing dozens of separate company-run lines under one unified, government-owned system.
1969
The Rajdhani Express was introduced between New Delhi and Howrah, India's first fully air-conditioned long-distance superfast train.
1988
The Shatabdi Express made its debut, offering same-day intercity travel between major business hubs.
2019
The South Coast Railway (SCoR) zone, headquartered in Visakhapatnam, was announced as the network's newest administrative zone.
2019
Vande Bharat Express, India's first indigenously manufactured semi-high-speed train, entered service.
2025
Indian Railways completed near-total electrification of its broad-gauge network and unveiled its first 9,000 HP electric locomotive, while the Mumbai–Ahmedabad High-Speed Rail Corridor continued active construction.

📏 Track Gauges Explained

Not all Indian Railways tracks are the same width. Understanding gauge is fundamental for any railfan, since it determines which rolling stock can run on which lines.

Broad Gauge (BG)
1,676 mm (5 ft 6 in)
The dominant gauge across India today, carrying almost all express, mail, and superfast services. Most of the network has now been converted to broad gauge under "Project Unigauge."
Metre Gauge (MG)
1,000 mm
Once widespread, now largely phased out or converted to broad gauge, except for a few heritage and tourist lines.
Narrow Gauge (NG)
610 mm / 762 mm
Survives mainly on UNESCO-listed heritage mountain lines like the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, Nilgiri Mountain Railway, and Kalka–Shimla Railway.

🚂 Locomotives: From Steam to 9,000 HP Electric

Locomotive classification in India follows a naming convention that tells you the gauge, power source, and purpose at a glance. For example, WAP-7 breaks down as: W (broad gauge), A (AC electric), P (passenger), 7 (model series).

Common Locomotive Classes

  • WAP-4 / WAP-5 / WAP-7 — Electric passenger locomotives, the backbone of express and Rajdhani/Shatabdi-class services.
  • WAG-9 / WAG-12 — Electric freight locomotives; the WAG-12 (in collaboration with Alstom) delivers very high horsepower for heavy freight corridors.
  • WDP-4 / WDG-4 — Diesel locomotives still used on non-electrified routes and for shunting/freight duties.
  • WCAM / WCG — Older dual or DC-compatible classes historically used on Mumbai-area lines before full AC conversion.

India's steam era, though officially retired from regular service decades ago, lives on through heritage runs. Locomotives like the WP class ("Pacific" type) and narrow-gauge engines on the Darjeeling and Nilgiri lines remain working pieces of railway history, lovingly maintained for tourists and enthusiasts.

📖 Railfan Glossary

Terms you'll often hear in railfan communities and on this Site:

  • Rake — The full set of coaches that make up a train.
  • LHB Coach — Linke Hofmann Busch coaches; modern, safer, and smoother-riding than older ICF coaches.
  • ICF Coach — Integral Coach Factory coaches; the older, traditional Indian coach design.
  • Loco Pilot — The official term for a train driver.
  • Chord Line — A shorter bypass track connecting two points on a railway, often used to avoid a busy junction (e.g. the Howrah Chord Line via Dankuni, bypassing Howrah Junction itself).
  • Pairing / Twin Pantograph — Refers to electric locomotive pantograph configurations used to draw power from overhead lines.
  • Zonal Railway — One of the 19 administrative zones into which Indian Railways divides its operations.
  • Spot (railfan slang) — To photograph or personally observe a specific train or locomotive at a location.

⚠️ A Note on Accuracy

Indian Railways is an enormous, constantly evolving system — new trains launch, zones occasionally get redrawn, and records get broken. We do our best to keep the information on this page current, but for live train status, PNR enquiry, or ticket booking, always refer to official sources like indianrail.gov.in or IRCTC.
